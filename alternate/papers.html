System 1 for Co-consult05

Abstract

Our objective is to solve some of the hardest problems in travel with frontier AI. How can frequent travel decisions become faster without quietly accepting confident errors, missing alternatives or unauthorised actions?

Co-consult’s travel work contains many small decisions inside a much larger task. Waypoint explores a System 1 layer for those decisions: a non-generative model scores supplied options, reports confidence and supports escalation to a language model or consultant. This paper reviews its two model designs, saved evaluation results and the gap between an offline escalation policy and the serving contract. Decide performs well on several synthetic Co-consult tasks, but results vary by use case, shortlist construction and phrasing. The contribution is a bounded integration model: use fast option scoring where the evidence supports it, keep hard constraints and action authority outside the model, and treat escalation as an explicit application responsibility.

The central idea

Give frequent, bounded decisions a fast path. Keep uncertainty, context and permission visible.

Keel: a governed kernel for travel agents01

Abstract

Keel addresses the production infrastructure needed to solve hard travel problems with agents. A declarative agent.yaml manifest compiles into a complete Model Context Protocol agent: gateway orchestration, skills, flows, entities, staged disclosure, interactive interfaces, authentication, interceptors, policy, state, audit, evaluations and packaging. This paper adapts the complete Keel chapter of the source implementation report, including its formal definitions, capability states and engineering examples.

ATP: the Agentic Travel Protocol03

Abstract

ATP addresses travel work that spans changing inventory, independent suppliers, scoped authority and two payment rails. This paper develops the implementation contract for draft v0.3: five wire operations, a compensating booking saga, TravellerToken and consent gates, supplier conformance and capability declarations, per-leg payment instructions, margin policy and reconciliation. It expands the original implementation-report chapter using the supplied ATP and payments documentation, with selected code inspection to distinguish documented intent from configuration-dependent behavior. No new empirical results are claimed.

ATFGraph: Keeping the Promise02

Abstract

Our objective is to solve some of the hardest problems in travel with frontier AI. How can a travel job remain coherent when workers restart, suppliers reply late and people approve actions hours later?

An agent’s work may outlast a model response, a worker process or a human approval. atfgraph provides a shared runtime for that work: it routes calls, records execution, schedules attempts, preserves state and supports decisions made later. This paper examines the supplied atfgraph snapshot as a framework-independent execution service with a Python authoring surface. We explain how existing agent code fits inside it, how a paused run continues and what an execution graph can actually show. We then connect those runtime primitives to Studio’s brief-to-agent build process. The central constraint is that durable records do not make arbitrary outside actions happen exactly once. Reliable applications still need replay-aware code, appropriate instrumentation and explicit approval checks.

Read paper →

The central idea

Keep the agent’s framework. Give its work a durable identity, a place to wait and a record that can be inspected.

Agentic payments for travel04

Abstract

Our objective is to solve some of the hardest problems in travel with frontier AI. How can an agent spend within a traveller’s authority and account for every financial obligation when bookings and payments evolve separately?

An agent can have permission to book without having unlimited permission to spend. Travel payments also continue after checkout: holds expire, bookings change, captures are delayed and refunds arrive later. This working draft separates approval of the purchase from authority to initiate payment and from evidence that money moved. It proposes a reservation-bound mandate, describes the role of a virtual credit card (VCC), and formalises duplicate control and reconciliation. The supplied keel payment implementation grounds parts of the design. The equations define a candidate contract and evaluation criteria; they do not establish a new adopted payment standard or certify live issuer behaviour.

Read draft →

The central idea

Every payment should be explainable through an approved reservation, a bounded instruction and a verified financial outcome.

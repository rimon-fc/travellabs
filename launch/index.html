01

Keel

Open source → paid cloud

Make any business usable by an AI agent.

Turn an API or document into checked tools. Include operators that work through websites or messages, with confirmation before a booking is treated as secured.

Free: the engine, rules, sign-in, records, tests and safety checks. Paid: managed hosting, protected credentials, team controls, alerts and API-change support. See Keel Cloud as a business ↓

What to know +

The developer win: connect an existing service and get a useful tool quickly. MCP is the common connection that lets AI applications use these tools. MCP Apps can also provide an interface for a person to review and approve.

02

ATFGraph

Open source → paid cloud

Keep agents working, even when work stops.

Run agents from any framework. Save progress, wait for approval, recover after interruption and keep a record of what happened.

Free: the engine, saved steps, memory and wake-ups, run on your own servers. Paid: managed operation, team access, approvals, watch-first mode, history and support. See ATFGraph Cloud as a business ↓

What to know +

The developer win: bring an existing agent and resume its work after a restart. harness-env is part of the supporting developer toolkit.

03

ATP

Open protocol → paid services

Give travel agents shared rules to book and pay.

A proposed Agentic Travel Protocol for permission, booking, payment and recovery. A failed trip must still show which bookings or payments need attention.

Open specification, schemas and reference examples. Commercial value comes through the Keel and ATFGraph offerings; ATP is not listed as a separate services business.

What to know +

Agentic Payment for Travel uses a virtual credit card (VCC) with defined spending limits. ATP could position FCTG to help shape how agents transact travel globally. The supplied paper reviews an alpha; it does not establish industry-wide adoption or universal recovery.

04

Waypoint

Innovation showcase🤗 Hugging Face · private models

Make the small decisions instantly.

FCTG’s decision model chooses from a supplied set of options. It handles routine choices and sends uncertain cases to a larger AI model or a consultant.

Faster responses and fewer expensive AI calls. The models and training data are hosted privately on Hugging Face.

What to know +

Reported tests: about 10 ms on an M4 Pro laptop. At the chosen thresholds, it handled 77% of Co-Consult test decisions at 96% accuracy. These are supplied test results, not live customer outcomes. It can still choose incorrectly; unfamiliar situations need escalation.

harness-env

A supporting repository in the TravelLabs developer toolkit, bringing setup, examples and documentation together.

A useful starting point gives more developers a reason to discover the organisation and try its core products.

What to know +

Give developers a clear starting point and a first example they can run. No separate cloud product is assumed here.

06

Conductor

Supporting repository

AI software development, with human review.

Conductor: AI software development (AI SDLC): prepares fixes for human review. Agents investigate issues, propose changes and leave the decision to an engineer.

Attract engineering teams with useful examples. First prove time saved in the lab, then test demand for managed team services.

What to know +

Keep human ownership of what ships. Track time to a useful proposed fix and whether the reviewer accepts it.

07

Ctrl-plane

Supporting repository

Keep routine AI work organised.

Ctrl-plane: organises routine AI work and background workers. Give people a clear view of what is running and what needs their attention.

Attract teams supervising ongoing agent work. Explore paid operational features after repeated use is established.

What to know +

Make responsibility, permissions and unfinished work visible. Test the workflows internally before broader release.

08

Opentravel

Supporting repository

Plan a trip on one visual board.

Opentravel: a visual trip-planning board for business users, like a Miro board. Bring ideas, itinerary parts and agent-assisted work into one place.

An approachable demonstration for consultants and business users. Test whether teams value a hosted shared workspace.

What to know +

Show a complete trip-planning example. Keep planning and suggestions distinct from confirmed bookings.

What it solves: AI buyers and travel sellers need separate connections and agreements. Neither side has an easy shared place to transact.

TAM calculation: 20,000,000–100,000,000 completed transactions × A$2. 20–100 million annual transactions that could use an agent-to-business travel exchange assumed. The proposed market is not yet established.

SAM calculation: 500,000–3,000,000 completed transactions × A$2. A small network of participating sellers and AI buyers: 0.5–3 million eligible transactions per year assumed, once membership is established.

Year 1 → 2 → 3 base volumes: 0 → 50,000 → 250,000 completed transactions. Multiply each by A$2 to get the upper value shown. The lower case is half that volume at the same price.

09Voice & Concierge
Voice & Concierge

Incubating · concept and interactive preview

A receptionist that helps when the team is busy.

The problem Calls and guest messages arrive after hours and during busy periods. Staff repeat the same answers, enquiries go unanswered and requests get lost between channels.

What it is An AI receptionist for hotels, holiday stays and travel businesses. It answers calls and messages using approved information, helps with everyday requests and shares the conversation with staff in one inbox.

Intended outcome Fewer missed enquiries, faster answers and more staff time for guests who need a person. These benefits must be tested in a pilot.

For example A guest calls at 2am about a delayed arrival. The assistant explains the approved arrival process and prepares a note for reception. A request outside its rules goes to a person.

Who pays
Hotel operators, holiday-stay managers and travel businesses.
How it could earn
A proposed subscription per property or team, with usage-based charges for calls and messages. Setup and integration support could be charged separately. Prices are not yet set.
Validate the opportunity before modelling revenue.

TAM, SAM and Year 1–3 revenue are not yet estimated. This idea is excluded from the portfolio’s financial totals.

First establish willingness to pay, call and message volumes, delivery cost, integration effort and the share of requests resolved well without staff help.

Proposed pilot, success measures & launch gates +

Start small: One property or travel team, one channel and one recurring need, such as after-hours arrival questions. Choose a sponsor and the staff member responsible for handoffs.

Prepare: Collect approved answers, define what the assistant may do and connect only the tools needed for the pilot. Test difficult questions and requests for a person with staff first.

Measure: Compare missed enquiries, response times, answer quality, successful staff handoffs and routine staff workload with a baseline. Track cost per resolved request and willingness to pay.

Commercial launch gate: Expand only after the pilot shows useful, accurate service, dependable handoffs and acceptable delivery costs. Agree paid terms before treating it as a commercial service.

Dependencies: A selected phone or messaging provider, approved business information, operating procedures and staff coverage for escalations. Booking actions would require a separate connected system and explicit permissions.

Current status: The website contains illustrative conversations, an interactive inbox and a pilot-brief builder. It does not make live calls, send guest messages or change bookings.

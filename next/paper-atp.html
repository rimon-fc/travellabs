Abstract

ATP addresses travel work that spans changing inventory, independent suppliers, scoped authority and two payment rails. This paper develops the implementation contract for draft v0.3: five wire operations, a compensating booking saga, TravellerToken and consent gates, supplier conformance and capability declarations, per-leg payment instructions, margin policy and reconciliation. It expands the original implementation-report chapter using the supplied ATP and payments documentation, with selected code inspection to distinguish documented intent from configuration-dependent behavior. No new empirical results are claimed.

Overview

Travel booking is a coordination problem over independently changing inventory, prices, approval, supplier commitments and money. A useful protocol must preserve those distinctions after the conversation ends. ATP, the Agentic Travel Protocol extension for Keel, addresses this problem through explicit operation contracts, reservation states, a compensating transaction coordinator and linked payment instructions. [1] [2]

ATP is alpha, targeting draft specification v0.3. Its operation names, fields and errors may change. The manifest pins atp.version: "0.3"; keel seams reports the capabilities present in a particular build. A protocol declaration, an executable supplier binding and a confirmed external booking are three different things.

This paper expands the original implementation-report chapter with the supplied ATP and payments documentation. Selected implementation paths were read to resolve the meaning of consent, capture ordering and recovery. No live supplier calls, payment transactions or new benchmark runs were performed for this revision. Documentation-level contracts are distinguished from guarantees that require provider behavior or deployment-specific verification.

1. The booking saga as compensable transactions

A multi-supplier journey cannot generally be committed as one shared ACID transaction. ATP uses a TripTransaction saga: reservations are held, legs are confirmed in order, and a failure initiates compensating cancellations in reverse order. The coordinator is an explicit coroutine with a try/except unwind rather than a flow graph whose early exception could skip cleanup.

The safety objective is that consent precedes commitment on every successful path. If cc denotes the relevant consent event, the intended condition is:

∀π∈Paths⁡(search→commit),c∈π. \forall\pi\in\operatorname{Paths}(\mathrm{search}\rightarrow\mathrm{commit}),\quad c\in\pi.

This is an authorization objective, not a claim that every deployment establishes an authenticated human decision. The implementation has a configurable token validator and a separate consent-construct gate. The origin and validity of the consent signal remain part of the deployment contract, discussed in Section 5.

The distinction between a supplier confirmation and the transaction’s terminal status matters for payment ordering. In the inspected _run_commit path, supplier legs are confirmed first. If those calls succeed, _capture_on_commit runs before the transaction is marked CONFIRMED. Thus “before the final confirm” in the documentation means before the final transaction-state transition, not before every supplier confirmation. A payment failure compensates the already-confirmed legs.

When a tasks engine is attached, the coordinator can submit commit as a pollable task. Task durability does not, by itself, establish restart-safe reconstruction of every supplier obligation: transaction state, effect identity and external outcomes must all be preserved. Cancellation is another external operation, potentially with fees, delays or failure. A terminal transaction status is therefore not sufficient evidence that every obligation has disappeared.

2. Reference implementation and protocol posture

The original report identifies travel-commerce as the supplied ATP reference implementation, combining supplier bindings, a booking workflow and business policy. It describes Co-consult as a consultation agent whose adoption of ATP as a live booking reference remains future work. Those roles are retained here without treating planned integration as deployed capability.

ATP is a standard in formation. Supplier capability declarations, operation-level errors and inspectable state make integration expectations explicit. Distribution and post-booking mechanisms are adjacent concerns: the supplied documentation states that UCP declarations derive from the same supplier mappings. Neither a discovery listing nor an advertised tier proves that an endpoint can complete a purchase. Detailed distribution behavior belongs to the separate distribution specification, beyond the two documents adapted here.

3. Payments are part of the booking contract

Booking creates two distinct financial obligations. On the consumer rail, the traveller pays the agent or merchant. On the supplier rail, payment is routed to the supplier through a virtual card, BSP settlement or direct transfer, according to the leg’s distribution channel. The two rails share reservation identity but require separate evidence.

One PaymentInstruction records each reservation leg. Capture, supplier payment, margin and reconciliation are represented explicitly rather than compressed into a single “paid” flag. A card’s ceiling is based on supplier cost, while the traveller-facing gross amount determines the consumer side. Margin is published as policy context so that a configured floor can reject an economically invalid commit.

Configuration groups consumer, supplier and margin under atp.payment. The documented consumer provider choices are acp, visa_tap and internal; the supplier choices are virtual_card, visa_tap_b2b and mpp. Endpoint configuration, implemented behavior and the reported seam state must be checked separately from those labels. Sections 9–11 describe capture timing, issuer settings and margin-policy wiring.

The implementation replays a previously stored capture result for the same payment intent. That mechanism is narrower than an unconditional exactly-once charge guarantee: the inspected path reads local state, performs the provider call and then stores the result. Concurrency, a lost reply or a crash between the external effect and local persistence require a provider-side idempotency and reconciliation contract. Section 9 states this boundary explicitly.

4. Configuration and the five wire operations

ATP ships as keel.ext.atp. Installing keel[atp] is the supported opt-in and adds no extra dependencies in the supplied documentation. Registration remains off unless atp.enabled is true. A minimal manifest fragment is:

atp:
  enabled: true
  version: "0.3"

Five ATP meta-tools are registered. Logical names use a colon; the documented FastMCP wire surface uses an underscore instead. Supplier operations (search, quote, hold, commit, cancel) are a separate layer from these coordinator tools.

Wire tool Scope Contract
atp_SearchOffers None Token-free inventory search and normalization into ATP offers.
atp_CreateTripTransaction hold Create an OPEN multi-supplier transaction.
atp_CreateReservation hold Add a held flight, hotel or activity leg; return an MCP reservation resource.
atp_CommitTripTransaction commit Run the consent-gated confirmation saga, compensating after partial failure.
atp_CancelReservation cancel Request a compensating cancellation for one leg.

The reservation resource has media type application/atp-reservation+json. Although the wire-tool summary in the documentation says “atomically confirm all legs,” the documented mechanism is a saga with compensation. It does not provide a distributed atomic commit across independent suppliers.

5. TravellerToken, consent and payment mandates

With verification configured, a TravellerToken is a signed JWT granting scoped authority. The validator checks signature, expiry, issuer and audience against configured JWKS or a public key; bounds the token lifetime; verifies the required operation scope; checks the commit amount; and prevents reuse of commit authority. Token lifetime is measured as exp - iat, with max_ttl clamped to a maximum of 86,400 seconds.

Boundary Documented check Failure or qualification
Authenticity Signature, exp, issuer and audience Verification material must actually be configured.
Lifetime exp - iat <= max_ttl, at most 24 hours A fresh invocation does not extend token validity.
Scope Required scope belongs to atp.scope ATP_TOKEN_INSUFFICIENT_SCOPE.
Value Commit total does not exceed atp.max_trip_value ATP_TOKEN_AMOUNT_EXCEEDED.
Replay Record commit use by jti or a token digest Reuse is refused with ATP_CONSENT_REQUIRED.

The documentation describes a durable SQLite spent-token registry. The inspected checkout additionally selects a shared key-value commit-token store for a Postgres-family state backend, while consulting an existing legacy SQLite registry. The storage choice is version- and configuration-dependent; it should not be described as universally SQLite-backed.

atp:
  traveller_token:
    issuer: https://idp.example.com
    jwks_uri: https://idp.example.com/.well-known/jwks.json
    max_ttl: 86400

Environment fallbacks are ATP_TOKEN_ISSUER, ATP_JWKS_URI or JWKS_URI, ATP_TOKEN_PUBLIC_KEY and ATP_TOKEN_AUDIENCE. Without verification material, the documented offline posture leaves the token gate open. The inspected harness returns no validator when neither JWKS nor public-key material is available, and also returns no validator if validator construction raises. Merely naming an issuer is not evidence that cryptographic verification is active. The consent prerequisite remains a separate mechanism.

Commit is gated by a consent construct whose default schema requires an approvedBy field. The gate accepts either an explicit signal under consent, consent_signal, consentToken or consent_token, or session state satisfying the consent construct. For example, state_set("consent", {"approvedBy": "jane"}) supplies the documented state shape. A manifest-defined construct overrides the default schema. The atp_consent_gate validator interceptor repeats the gate before saga execution.

In the inspected code, the explicit-argument check tests whether one of those signals is truthy. A field named approvedBy or a truthy argument does not independently authenticate a person. A deployment claiming human authorization must bind these inputs to a trusted approval process and the reviewed itinerary. The paper therefore does not equate presence of a consent signal with proof of informed consent.

An unmet construct produces KEEL_PREREQUISITES_UNMET, with stage commit, code -32107 and missing_prerequisites identifying consent. The caller satisfies the named construct and retries. An optional ap2_mandate JWT claim supplies payment scope and amount constraints. Once published as session policy facts, these constraints are implicit deny rules before ATP booking tools; a present, non-object claim is rejected with ATP_CONSENT_REQUIRED.

6. Supplier bindings and capability honesty

Each entry in atp.suppliers binds a logical supplier to a servers: entry through a validated foreign key. Its ops map translates logical operations into actual upstream tool names. Binding defaults are merged underneath call arguments; explicitly supplied values take precedence. The default normalizer attaches supplier identity and retains the raw response under source, rather than manufacturing unavailable inventory details.

atp:
  suppliers:
    ean:
      server: hotels
      conformance_tier: L1
      ops:
        search: ean_hotel_search
        quote: ean_room_availability
      defaults:
        currency: AUD

This fragment assumes a separately configured hotels server. Bound operations use MCPSupplierAdapter and gateway.call_tool with the wire name {server}__{tool}, through the gateway’s normal interceptor path. Servers lacking a binding retain the raw dispatch fallback described by the documentation.

Logical operation Minimum declared tier What the declaration does not prove
search, quote L1: Search Current inventory or a supplier hold.
hold, cancel L2: Hold A mapped and reachable supplier endpoint for every operation.
commit L3: Atomic commit Atomic commitment across all suppliers in the itinerary.

An unmapped operation returns an ATP_OP_NOT_SUPPORTED envelope with supplier, operation, tier, seam and a specific mapping instruction; its JSON-RPC transport code is -32601. For supported coordinator-owned handling of an unmapped booking operation below the required supplier tier, the result is an internal record marked coordinatorOwned: true and supplierConfirmed: false, with the unsupported-operation detail preserved. A configuration gap at a sufficient tier remains a refusal. harness.supplier_capabilities() exposes mapped, tool, min_tier and coordinator_owned per operation.

This distinction is commercially significant. “HELD” in a coordinator record must not be presented as supplier-held inventory without supplier evidence. An integration must inspect the capability and confirmation fields, not infer success from a booking-shaped object.

7. Reservation states, partial failure and recovery

A reservation leg progresses through SEARCHING, HELD, CONFIRMED and potentially COMPLETED. A held leg can expire; cancellation and compensation use COMPENSATING and CANCELLED. Illegal transitions are rejected. An elapsed hold TTL raises ATP_HOLD_EXPIRED and requires a renewed reservation rather than reuse of the expired promise.

At transaction level the main path is OPEN → COMMITTING → CONFIRMED. Failure moves through COMPENSATING to CANCELLED. The coordinator visits confirmed legs in reverse order, attempting every compensating cancellation even if another cancellation fails. A failed compensation retains per-leg error detail and may leave that leg COMPENSATING; it does not vanish when the parent transaction becomes CANCELLED.

ATP_COMMIT_PARTIAL_FAILURE carries the full transaction in its error data, including leg statuses. If capture fails, captureFailedAt and captureError are recorded and the same compensation path is used. An operator can therefore distinguish a failed confirmation, a completed compensation and an unresolved compensation. Operational closure requires accounting for the remaining supplier and financial obligations, not simply observing the parent terminal state.

8. PaymentInstruction: the per-leg record

The shared StateStore persists each instruction under atp:payment:instruction:<id>. It ties a paymentInstructionId to a reservationId and tripTransactionId, with separate consumer, supplier, margin, reconciliation and audit blocks.

Block Principal fields Meaning
consumer_payment Provider, intent, amount/currency, status, capture time Collection from the traveller.
supplier_payment Merchant of record, method, card, settlement Payment to the supplier and expected settlement details.
margin Gross, cost, net, percentage Commercial facts used by policy.
reconciliation PNR, booking reference, card reference, consultant, branch Correlation between booking and financial records.
audit Transition history Previous state, next state, time and reason.

The documented lifecycle is DRAFT → ISSUED → CAPTURED → SETTLED → RECONCILED, with failure transitions to FAILED. Each transition updates audit.transitions and attempts an atp_payment audit append. The inspected builder starts card-backed instructions as ISSUED and other instructions as DRAFT; applications should not assume all channel types begin in the same state.

The documentation’s example has gross AUD 750, supplier cost AUD 600, net AUD 150 and margin 20%. These are illustrative amounts, not measured commercial performance. The virtual-card ceiling is AUD 600 in that example. Booking, payment-instruction and payment-intent identifiers serve different purposes and should not be collapsed into a single identifier.

9. Consumer capture and the replay boundary

atp.payment.consumer selects the consumer rail. ACP capture posts to acp_checkout_url with the intent, amount and itinerary as a structured product. The product schema defaults to atp.itinerary. With no checkout endpoint, the engine returns KEEL_PAYMENT_NOT_CONFIGURED, identifying seam atp.payment.consumer and guard acp_checkout_url.

The internal provider records capture on the agent’s internal ledger without calling an external processor; that ledger event is not independent proof of money moving at a bank. The visa_tap option is declared but contract-only in this checkout and returns KEEL_CONTRACT_ONLY. These capability states are implementation-specific, not statements about the availability of an external payment standard generally.

The documented capture_on values are commit and hold, with commit the default. The commit hook acts only when the payment engine is attached and the setting is commit. A missing engine retains the pre-payment saga behavior. The inspected commit hook skips capture for a different value; the configuration enum alone does not prove that a working hold-time hook is installed.

Payment-intent identity is selected from an explicit payment_intent_id, the transaction’s payment token, or derived intent_<txn_id>. A stored result at atp:payment:capture:<intent> is returned with replayed: true. The intended replay property is:

stored⁡(k)⇒retry⁡(k)=recordedResult⁡(k). \operatorname{stored}(k)\Rightarrow \operatorname{retry}(k)=\operatorname{recordedResult}(k).

That implication is conditional on persistence. The read–provider-call–write sequence inspected here does not itself supply an atomic claim over concurrent callers or close the crash window following a successful provider call. Stronger exactly-once charging requires evidence about provider idempotency, atomic ownership, recovery and reconciliation. The previous paper’s unconditional “captures are exactly-once” statement is therefore replaced by this narrower contract.

10. Supplier routing and virtual-card controls

The reservation’s channel, segment.channel or supplier.channel selects merchant-of-record routing. Unknown channels default to SUPPLIER_DIRECT in the documented implementation.

Channel Merchant of record Method Settlement mechanism
AIRLINE_DIRECT Airline bsp BSP calendar.
OTA Agent virtual_card Supplier charges the issued card.
SUPPLIER_DIRECT Supplier direct Direct transfer.

Settlement metadata lives under supplier_payment.settlement, with mechanism, expected_at and reference. Selecting a method describes the instruction’s routing; it is not evidence that the corresponding settlement has occurred.

For OTA legs, card issuance posts to atp.payment.supplier.virtual_card.issuer. The requested amount ceiling is pricing.cost, falling back to pricing.total when cost is absent. Validity defaults to 21 days and single_use defaults to true; the flag is forwarded to the issuer. The returned reference is copied into both the supplier-payment and reconciliation blocks. Issuer-side enforcement of these requested controls remains part of the integration contract.

With no issuer, an OTA instruction returns the guarded KEEL_PAYMENT_NOT_CONFIGURED envelope instead of constructing a card-less instruction. Transport failures and non-2xx responses raise PaymentError. register_mpp_intent is explicitly contract-only in this checkout: it returns KEEL_CONTRACT_ONLY and audits the attempted use. A configured provider name is not equivalent to an implemented rail.

11. Margin as policy context

compute_margin calculates gross less supplier cost and stores margin_pct and total_amount under the policy-context provider name margin_calculator. For positive gross gg and supplier cost ss:

net=g−s,margin_pct=100g−sg. \mathrm{net}=g-s,\qquad \mathrm{margin\_pct}=100\frac{g-s}{g}.

The inspected function returns a zero percentage when gross is zero. A margin-floor configuration is not sufficient in isolation: the manifest must wire the context provider and the relevant deny rule. The documented arrangement is:

atp:
  payment:
    margin:
      floor_pct: 5.0
      audit: true
policies:
  engine: yaml_rules
  context_providers: [margin_calculator]
  rules:
    - id: margin_floor
      "on": [atp_CommitTripTransaction]
      when: facts.margin_pct < rules.min_margin_pct
      action: deny
      error_code: POLICY_MARGIN_FLOOR

The supplied documentation describes this deny rule as failing closed when the margin cannot be verified. margin.calculator_flow may name an overriding computation, and margin.audit defaults to true. These are policy integration requirements: missing context, the installed rule set and the actual commit path must be covered by evaluation before treating the margin floor as an enforced guarantee.

12. PII handling, audit and reconciliation

The atp_pii_redactor mutator interceptor removes configured PII paths; the documented examples are passengerDetails.passportNumber and travellerToken. Redaction scope is configured, so it should not be described as removing every possible passenger identifier from every downstream record.

With audit_all_transitions: true, the documented booking path emits an immutable observability ExecutionRecord of kind atp_transition and appends to the hash-chained audit sink. The configured observability.audit_sink is used where available; otherwise the default sink honors KEEL_AUDIT_DB. Payment transitions use atp_payment and also maintain the instruction’s own transition history.

emit_reconciliation_event emits an observability record when that engine is attached, appends an atp_reconciliation record containing PNR, booking reference, virtual-card reference, consultant ID and branch code, and transitions the instruction to RECONCILED. This connects the records needed for back-office matching. Calling the method is not, by itself, a proof that independent processor and supplier evidence agrees: the inspected function emits the supplied correlation fields and updates status.

Hash chaining supports verification of the retained sequence. It does not establish completeness when an append fails or independently prevent deletion of an unanchored history. The inspected payment audit helper catches append failures so that they do not break the payment operation. Consequently, audit availability, trusted retention and external evidence remain operational requirements; the paper does not claim that every payment operation is transactionally coupled to an audit append.

13. Error contracts

The ATP error namespace distinguishes changed inventory, missing authority and unresolved execution. Callers should use the structured code and payload rather than interpret a generic booking-shaped response as success.

Code Symbol Meaning
-32100 ATP_INVENTORY_GONE Inventory disappeared after search.
-32101 ATP_HOLD_EXPIRED Hold TTL elapsed.
-32102 ATP_PRICE_CHANGED Price changed after pricing.
-32103 ATP_TOKEN_INSUFFICIENT_SCOPE Required token scope is absent.
-32104 ATP_TOKEN_AMOUNT_EXCEEDED Trip total exceeds the granted ceiling.
-32105 ATP_COMMIT_PARTIAL_FAILURE Partial confirmation triggered compensation; inspect transaction data.
-32106 ATP_SUPPLIER_UNAVAILABLE Supplier timeout or unreachable endpoint.
-32107 ATP_CONSENT_REQUIRED Missing consent, invalid mandate shape or refused commit-token reuse, depending on the gate.
-32108 ATP_LOYALTY_INVALID Supplier rejected a loyalty identifier.
-32109 ATP_DOCUMENT_NOT_READY Confirmation exists but the document is not ready.

These differ from adapter-level ATP_OP_NOT_SUPPORTED (-32601), KEEL_PAYMENT_NOT_CONFIGURED, and KEEL_CONTRACT_ONLY. An unavailable capability is not a confirmed booking or captured payment and should remain visible to the caller.

14. Evaluation scope and remaining obligations

The supplied documentation describes a chaos harness that injects failure at each leg-confirm and compensation position, including compensation failure. Its asserted properties are terminal parent transaction state, reverse-order compensation, legal reservation transitions, an explanatory audit record for unresolved confirmed or compensating legs, and successful audit-chain verification. These are descriptions of the supplied tests; this revision did not execute them or infer production reliability from their existence.

The guarded live-supplier test is enabled with KEEL_LIVE_SUPPLIERS=1. It connects L1 flight and hotel MCP endpoints, normally ports 8011 and 8010, and checks normalization into ATP offers. Without the flag it skips. Search normalization coverage is distinct from live hold, commit, payment capture, cancellation and restart recovery.

The central research objective remains solving the hardest operational problems in travel: preserving authority when terms change, coordinating partial commitments, recovering from ambiguous outcomes and reconciling two financial rails. A useful evaluation must cross those boundaries. Future integration evidence should exercise duplicate requests, expiry, missing verification material, compensation failure, lost payment replies, unavailable audit storage and independently verified settlement. Those are proposed validation obligations, not results claimed by this paper.

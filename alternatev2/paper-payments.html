Abstract

Our objective is to solve some of the hardest problems in travel with frontier AI. How can an agent spend within a traveller’s authority and account for every financial obligation when bookings and payments evolve separately?

An agent can have permission to book without having unlimited permission to spend. Travel payments also continue after checkout: holds expire, bookings change, captures are delayed and refunds arrive later. This working draft separates approval of the purchase from authority to initiate payment and from evidence that money moved. It proposes a reservation-bound mandate, describes the role of a virtual credit card (VCC), and formalises duplicate control and reconciliation. The supplied keel payment implementation grounds parts of the design. The equations define a candidate contract and evaluation criteria; they do not establish a new adopted payment standard or certify live issuer behaviour.

THE CENTRAL IDEA Every payment should be explainable through an approved reservation, a bounded instruction and a verified financial outcome.

1. Travel changes after the payment button

A reservation can be held, confirmed, changed or cancelled. Payment can be authorised, captured, settled or refunded on a different timetable. Collapsing those events into “paid” hides the conditions an agent must respect.

This draft is separate from ATP’s trip-coordination paper. ATP describes the permission and lifecycle of the trip; this paper focuses on the authority and evidence behind its payment instructions. A cancellation record does not establish that a refund has arrived, just as an issued card does not establish that a charge succeeded.

2. Travel payments are a coupled-ledger problem

The objective is to solve a hard travel-specific coordination problem: the traveller’s approved purchase, the merchant’s collection and the supplier’s settlement do not necessarily complete together. Rebooking can create a replacement charge while a refund remains pending; partial cancellation can leave a valid residual cost. A single “paid” indicator cannot represent that state faithfully.

The public platform report distinguishes a consumer rail, through which the traveller pays, and a supplier rail, through which each reservation is funded. It describes supplier-cost virtual-card limits and deterministic margin facts. Those mechanisms belong to different scopes: a card ceiling limits an instrument, margin policy evaluates a commercial condition, and purchase approval binds what the traveller agreed to buy.

We use the term mandate for the candidate application contract in this paper. It is not a claim to introduce an adopted banking standard. The correctness target is an auditable relationship among approved reservation, bounded instruction, participant receipt and reconciled ledger.

3. Three boundaries of authority

Purchase approval establishes the proposed trip and its material terms. Payment authority limits who may pay, what the payment is for and how much it may spend. Payment evidence records what the payment system actually did. Each has a separate job.

Agentic Payment for Travel can use a virtual credit card (VCC) with a defined amount ceiling, validity and use limit. The supplied snapshot requests these controls from a configured issuer and records the card reference beside the booking. It does not turn a card into proof that the room or itinerary matches the approved purchase.

A changed purchase may require fresh approval even when it remains below the payment ceiling. A payment instrument must not be treated as a substitute for the travel decision.

Checking a counter and spending are not automatically one atomic action. Concurrent attempts need enforcement at the issuer or payment boundary; application checks alone cannot guarantee single use. Card controls also need to be confirmed by the issuer rather than inferred from a request to create a card.

The reviewed code returns a saved capture result for an already recorded intent. That is narrower than proving P3 for every crash, concurrent request and provider response. The provider must participate in duplicate control, or the application must query an authoritative outcome before retrying an uncertain payment.

Ci,c, Ri,c
Verified captured funds and verified refunds for reservation i in currency c.
Ei,c, Δi,c

Use integer minor units and keep currencies separate. Authorisations that have not captured are not captured funds. Fees, foreign exchange and partial refunds must be represented explicitly in the agreed outcome and the relevant ledger; they must not be silently absorbed into a balancing number.

Evaluate P5 for every reservation and currency. Adding discrepancies across unrelated bookings can hide one overcharge behind another undercharge. Supplier settlement is a separate ledger to reconcile; matching the traveller’s ledger does not prove that settlement is complete.

5. Case study: money moved, but the reply was lost

ILLUSTRATIVE CASE

A timeout is not a declined payment.

A capture request is sent. The provider completes it, but the response never reaches the agent. The local record cannot yet tell whether the money moved. Sending a new request under a new identity may create a second effect.

The safe continuation preserves the original payment identity, queries or reconciles the outcome and retries only under a verified duplicate-control contract. Until that outcome is known, the interface should show that payment is being checked. It should not label the payment declined or successful merely to keep the conversation moving.

The code’s saved-result guard is useful once a result has been recorded. The difficult research case is the interval between the outside effect and that durable record. This draft proposes failure tests for that interval; it does not claim that the supplied guard alone closes it.

6. Changes, refunds and ongoing care

A cancellation should create a clear account of any expected refund, the evidence needed to confirm it and who follows up if it remains pending. A changed trip may involve a new payment and a later refund on the original booking. Those are separate obligations, not a single instant exchange.

Price-drop rebooking should retain the original booking until a suitable replacement is secured, subject to the applicable terms. Price-lock coverage additionally needs a responsible party and an explicit coverage contract. A saved amount, a VCC or a timer is not itself evidence of that protection.

The completion rule should follow the financial outcome the traveller was promised. It should make disputed, pending and unknown amounts visible without treating every delay as a failure.

8. Concurrency, ambiguity and duplicate control

Analytical proposition · stated assumptions

Check-then-spend is not single-use enforcement

Suppose two concurrent workers both observe an unused mandate before either records a successful use. If both may dispatch independently, a local eligibility predicate can admit two effects even when the use budget is one.

Argument. Both workers evaluate the same pre-effect state. Without an atomic reservation of the use budget, a serialising payment boundary or participant-enforced duplicate identity, neither observation excludes the other. The eligibility predicate is necessary but not sufficient.

The public platform report describes capture retries replaying a recorded result and uses exactly-once terminology. The inherited implementation review identifies a stored-result guard. We distinguish that documented mechanism from a stronger claim covering crashes before persistence, concurrent workers and ambiguous provider replies. The latter needs a specified provider contract and failure trials; it cannot be inferred from a saved-response branch alone.

Bind an operation identity to a canonical request payload. Repeating the same identity with different amount, currency or reservation should be treated as a conflict, not a new interpretation of the original instruction. Retain evidence of the conflict without exposing card data or unnecessary traveller information.

Revocation and expiry add a second race: authority may change between eligibility and capture. The enforcement contract must state when authority is evaluated and which participant guarantees the decision. An application timestamp alone does not establish that a delayed external charge remained authorised.

9. Questions for the next revision

Who issues payment authority, and who may revoke it? Which changes to a trip invalidate that authority? What evidence is sufficient to resolve an unknown charge? How are expiry, partial refunds and disputes represented across different providers?

The next step is a reviewed contract and independent implementation trials. A shared travel payment standard requires agreement on these boundaries and on responsibility when an outcome remains unknown. This working draft opens that discussion; it does not declare it settled.

10. Discussion and conclusion

Agentic travel payments require bounded authority and evidence that survives the changing trip. The proposed mandate, identity and reconciliation rules make spending obligations explicit. Their value must be established at the participant boundary, especially under concurrent requests, lost acknowledgements and delayed refunds.
